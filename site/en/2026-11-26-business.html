Does T-Bank's private ecosystem hit a state data wall?

The group is committing 77 billion roubles to the Cian platform. Yet state services return only 60% of usable data.

26 November 2026

T-Technologies has made its largest move yet to buy up private digital platforms. A voluntary buyout offer was submitted to shareholders of the real estate classifieds service Cian for up to 100% of the capital at 993.05 roubles per share. The bid includes a 29.3% premium over the November 5 market price, valuing the company's entire 77,670,490-share capital at 77.13 billion roubles. On this news, Cian shares on the Moscow Exchange jumped by more than 30% to 908.8 roubles apiece.

The buyer has little interest in a minor stake: the deal will close only if at least 50% of the capital, or 38,835,246 shares, is tendered. Applications from shareholders will be accepted until February 22, 2027, with settlement made exclusively in cash within 17 days after the window closes. T-Bank guarantees all financial obligations, having provided an irrevocable guarantee of 77.13 billion roubles. The transaction will be funded using internal and borrowed capital without issuing additional holding shares.

The acquisition of Cian continues the group's strategy of aggressive expansion. In June, T-Technologies bought the auto classifieds platform Auto.ru from Yandex for 35 billion roubles, also using borrowed funds. The sequential absorption of market-leading platforms turns the banking group into a universal ecosystem that controls the largest capital-intensive deal markets, from car purchases to real estate transactions.

However, the large-scale expansion of private ecosystems has run into a rigid external constraint for the first time. Having built a streamlined pipeline for customer acquisition and servicing, the private holding inevitably becomes hostage to state information services and infrastructure. The rapid integration of major platforms stumbles over the quality of state data that underpins modern scoring and identification.

The primary barrier to building a seamless ecosystem is the low quality of state data used for scoring and identification. According to Sergei Klimenko, deputy director for state partnership development at T-Bank, at best about 60% of information from a citizen's "Digital Profile" is valid for credit risk assessment. The Uniform Biometric System (UBS) presents an even stricter hurdle. Within the group's subsidiary microfinance company T-Finance, out of 1,400 real clients sent through this scenario, only 7% reached a final loan. An experiment to simplify the procedure—abandoning voice modality—proved entirely unviable: not a single one of the 10 bank employees managed to pass verification using facial recognition alone.

A full identification requirement via the UBS for online lending has formally applied to microfinance companies since March, yet the regulator was forced to withhold supervisory sanctions until January 1, 2027. The National Financial Market Council has already asked the Bank of Russia to extend the moratorium to January 1, 2028, citing delays in infrastructure adaptation at the Center for Biometric Technologies. Meanwhile, the group's financial commitments for expansion are already fixed: to secure the buyout offer for Cian shareholders, T-Bank issued an irrevocable guarantee of 77.13 billion roubles, valid from February 19 to October 2, 2027.

In parallel, the Central Bank is preparing further regulatory tightening that will directly affect the scoring systems of acquired platforms. Starting in April 2027, a "one loan per person" restriction will apply to products with a full cost of credit exceeding 100% per annum, and from September 1, 2027, lenders will be required to rely solely on official income data. Large traditional banks can use salary data from their own client base, but specialized platforms and microfinance lenders find themselves in a vulnerable position: low-quality data from state registries sharply reduces legal conversion flows, pushing some borrowers into the grey zone.

The state's attempt to thoroughly regulate scoring and whiten the retail lending market produces the exact opposite result. Excessively strict identification requirements and the technical immaturity of state systems stimulate demand for fast financing, driving citizens into the unregulated sector. In the first half of 2026 alone, the Bank of Russia identified about 1,000 illegal lenders, compared with 1,100 for the whole of 2025. The growth of the shadow segment demonstrates that while regulated ecosystems lose clients at the biometric stage, private capital moves to areas where state registries are not used at all.

Similar avoidance mechanics are visible in the behaviour of small businesses, which form the active base of commercial ecosystems. The primary catalyst for structural shifts was the lowering of the revenue threshold for VAT payers under the standard simplified tax regime to 20 million roubles starting in 2026. To maintain tax exemptions and avoid an avalanche-like rise in reporting, entrepreneurs began changing tax regimes en masse. The automated simplified taxation system, which preserves benefits up to a revenue threshold of 60 million roubles, serves as the main shelter.

According to a Federal Tax Service presentation, the number of users on the automated regime jumped tenfold in the first five months of 2026, reaching 444,000 entrepreneurs compared with 43,100 at the end of 2025. Analysts forecast the figure will reach 700,000 to 900,000 by the end of 2026, and up to 1 million users if integrated directly with marketplaces. The Finance Ministry guarantees the parameters of the experiment will remain unchanged until the end of 2027, but the explosive flight of businesses into automated tax niches confirms a general rule: any regulatory attempt to reach every rouble forces the economy to build workarounds.

In the battle for key services, private capital faces not only regulatory rigidity but also resistance from competitors. T-Technologies' announced buyout offer for Cian hinges on its ownership structure: Elbrus Capital founder Dmitry Kryukov controls 37.43% of the votes (32.98% of shares), while co-founders Dmitry Demin and Maxim Melnikov hold 8.12% and 7.08% respectively. With a free float of 40%, the group needs to gather at least 50% of the capital to execute the deal. The appearance of Alfa-Bank in negotiations, laying claim to the Elbrus Capital stake, turns the takeover into a fierce standoff where a 29.3% price premium no longer guarantees easy control.

The strategic calculation of T-Technologies, which spent 35 billion roubles on Auto.ru and guaranteed 77.13 billion roubles for the Cian offer, was built on the rapid integration of client flows. Yet group chief financial officer Igor Gerasimov stated plainly that no preliminary agreements had been reached with the service's shareholders before sending the documents. At the same time, the banking group is forced to wage a complex lobbying campaign: the National Financial Market Council's initiative to extend the moratorium on supervisory measures to January 1, 2028, underscores that the technical cycle of the Biometric Technologies Center simply cannot keep pace with the law.

As a result, an institutional wedge has formed between private investment plans and state regulatory pressure. Central Bank department director Ilya Kochetkov confirms the regulator's inflexibility regarding the enforcement of requirements in 2027, while the State Duma insists on tougher oversight of violations. Large businesses borrow capital and disburse tens of billions of roubles to consolidate platforms, yet the real return from these ecosystems remains trapped in the narrow bottleneck of state infrastructure that is unready for commercial loads.

However, the state's logic in tightening financial market control rests on clear objectives. Preventive measures against debt spirals, including the "one loan per person" rule for products with a full cost of credit above 100% per annum starting in April 2027, are designed to curb fraud risks. Yet the rule from September 1, 2027, obliging lenders to rely solely on official income data from state databases, founders on the technical imperfection of the "Digital Profile". When only about 60% of received data is recognized as valid for risk assessment, the regulatory filter begins blocking legal issuances.

This creates a direct conflict between the investment model of private ecosystems and the capabilities of state regulation. Directing tens of billions of roubles to market consolidation—from the 35 billion rouble buyout of Auto.ru to the 77.13 billion rouble offer to Cian shareholders—financial groups counted on the instant monetization of services through cross-selling credit products. In practice, scoring algorithms run into the requirements of the UBS and tax reports, collapsing process conversion to single-digit percentages and driving clients toward illegal lenders.

The expansion of commercial platforms has hit the limits of state digital institutions. The attempt to forcibly link fast banking scoring with immature data registries turns any major classifieds site into an isolated storefront asset. By buying market leaders, private capital assumes full regulatory risk: while state services provide questionable data, owning 100% of the capital of top platforms does not guarantee the expected return on invested billions.
